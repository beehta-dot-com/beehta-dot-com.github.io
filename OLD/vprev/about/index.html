One shelf, two sizes of thing.

What this is

Most software grows until it does everything badly. The idea here is the opposite: build one small thing that does its job properly, put it on its own address, and leave it alone. When a second thing is needed it becomes a second entry on the shelf, not a new tab inside the first.

beehta.com itself holds nothing. It is a catalogue. Everything listed lives on its own address and this page points at it.

Two kinds of thing, and why the difference matters

Roughly half the shelf is single pages. A break-even calculator, a numerology reckoner, a JSON formatter, an itinerary you can shuffle into order. They load in one request, they answer one question, and they are finished in the sense that a hammer is finished. There is no account, nothing is saved, and nothing you type into one is sent anywhere.

The rest are full web apps. A logins register, a portfolio, a trips planner, a review cycle, a small social network. These hold your work, so they need somewhere to put it, which means an account.

Every entry in the catalogue is labelled with which of the two it is, in the list and again on the entry itself. It is the first thing worth knowing about a thing you are about to open, so it is the first thing the page tells you.

One account, all of the apps

You sign in once, in one place, and that covers every app on the shelf. No app asks for a password of its own and none of them keeps its own list of users. There is a single sign-in page and everything else borrows from it.

So adding a new app does not mean another account to remember, and closing your account closes it everywhere at once. The pages are not part of this at all: they never see a sign-in because they have nothing to sign in to.

Try before you sign up

Every entry that exists has a small working preview on the home page. It is a stand-in, not the real thing: fewer features, no saving, nothing sent anywhere. It is there so you can tell in ten seconds whether the thing is any use to you, without handing over an email address first.

What it does not do

  • No adverts, anywhere.
  • No selling or sharing of what you put into an app.
  • No account asked for by something that does not need one.
  • No pushing you towards a paid tier you did not ask about.
  • No feature that exists only to make a chart go up.
Check these before publishing. Those five are promises. Keep only the ones you are certain every page and every app will honour, and see Privacy for what actually gets collected.
