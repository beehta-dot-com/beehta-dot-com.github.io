Pages and apps

Small pages for a quick answer. Small apps for the rest.

Some things you need once: a number worked out, a file tidied, a plan laid down. Those are single pages here. You open one, get what you came for, and close the tab. Nothing to sign up for.

Other things you come back to for years, and those are proper apps with your work kept in them. Both live on this shelf, and the list below says which is which.

Two kinds of thing

Not everything needs to be an app.

Most sites turn every small job into a product with a sign-up page in front of it. Half the things on this shelf do not need one, and the page says so rather than pretending otherwise.

Page

A single page that answers one question

A calculator, a generator, an editor. It loads in one request, does its job, and forgets you the moment the tab closes.

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • Nothing saved, and nothing sent to us.
  • Works with the tab offline once it has loaded.
App

A web app that keeps your work

Something you come back to: a portfolio, a set of trips, a review cycle. It holds your data, so it needs an account, and it says so on the entry.

  • One account covers every app on the shelf.
  • Your work stays inside the app you put it in.
  • Closing your account closes it everywhere at once.

How it works

Open it. Use it. Close it.

That is the whole shape of a page on this shelf. It runs in your browser, it answers the one question it was built for, and it keeps nothing. The apps ask for a little more, and only because they have to hold on to something for you.

Pick one and that is it. There is no onboarding here, because for half the shelf there is nothing to onboard into.

  1. 01

    Find it

    Search the list or filter it down to pages, apps, or one category. The list stays where it is while you look through it.

  2. 02

    Try it

    Every entry that exists has a working preview beside it. It costs nothing, asks for nothing, and tells you in ten seconds whether the thing is any use to you.

  3. 03

    Open it

    A page opens and works straight away. An app asks you to sign in once, in one place, and that same account covers everything else on the shelf.

Where this stops

What does not do.

Four promises, kept short enough to be checked.

  • No ads

    Not on this page, and not inside anything listed on it.

  • No feature bloat

    Every feature needs a reason to exist. A page that grows a second job becomes a second page.

  • No unnecessary accounts

    If a thing does not need to know who you are, it will not ask. That is why half the shelf has no sign-in at all.

  • No dark patterns

    No artificial urgency, no engagement tricks, no upgrade prompt behind a button you did not press.
